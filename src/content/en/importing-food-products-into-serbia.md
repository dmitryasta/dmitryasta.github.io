This article describes the import chain for food products into Serbia in the order in which a shipment moves. It is a practical map, not legal or customs advice: the exact requirements depend on the tariff code, the origin and the product category. Information is current as of 30 October 2026.

## 1. Who does what

- **Seller abroad.** Issues the commercial invoice and transport documents and, where needed, the proof of origin and the veterinary or phytosanitary certificate of the exporting country.
- **Importer in Serbia.** Buys or receives the goods, pays import VAT and duty, holds the veterinary decision where required and is named on the label.
- **Customs representative.** Under the Customs Law any person may appoint a customs representative. A representative must generally be established in Serbia, except where it acts for persons who need not be established there. Direct representation is in the principal's name, indirect representation in the representative's own name for the principal's account.
- **Freight forwarder.** Indirect representation may be performed by a legal entity with a registered seat in Serbia, registered for international freight forwarding, with at least one employee who holds a customs-representation permit.

A US agricultural report adds that foreign suppliers may export through a locally registered office, a local company or a registered shipping or forwarding agent. Choosing the model is covered in [how to enter the Serbian market](../how-to-enter-the-serbian-market/).

## 2. Customs: declaration, value, duty

The customs declaration is lodged by the declarant, who is responsible for its accuracy and completeness. Customs value is the transaction value. A customs debt on import arises when the declaration is accepted: the declarant is the debtor, and in indirect representation the person on whose behalf the declaration is made is also a debtor.

The duty rate depends on the tariff code and on the origin of the goods. The tariff follows the EU Combined Nomenclature. Duty rates come either from the Customs Tariff Law or from free trade agreements the country has signed. Published ranges differ by scope: a US report states third-country agricultural duties from 0 to 30 percent of import value, and a tax guide states 0 to 57.6 percent across all goods. Neither replaces reading the rate for your exact code.

## 3. VAT on import

Import is taxed at the general rate of 20 percent or at the special rate of 10 percent for goods on the list in the Law on VAT. The tax base for import is the customs value plus excise, duty and other import charges and the incidental costs up to the first destination in Serbia. A VAT-registered importer using the goods for business can recover import VAT. The Customs Administration list of 10 percent goods (status of 1 January 2024) includes meat, fish, dairy, eggs, vegetables, fruit, cereals, edible oils, sugar and bread products; it is a working guideline subject to revision, and the current list must be checked.

## 4. Product controls

Controls depend on the product and take place at border crossings that have inspection posts.

- **Veterinary.** For animal-origin food the importer applies to the Veterinary Directorate for a decision on veterinary-sanitary conditions before import. The decision is issued per product type and can cover consignments of that type for three months, extendable by three months. Each consignment needs the original International Veterinary Certificate signed by the official veterinarian of the exporting country and the Serbian decision. Customs may not clear the consignment until the border veterinary inspector confirms the controls. The producing establishment must meet the prescribed conditions: registered in the EU, or approved by the Ministry as equivalent.
- **Phytosanitary.** A US report states that origin-country phytosanitary certificates are required for plants, seeds, fruit and vegetables. The primary procedure could not be retrieved and should be confirmed.
- **Ministry of Agriculture approval.** Required for the categories listed in the FAQ above.
- **Sanitary.** The Ministry of Health controls novel foods, dietetic and baby foods, supplements, additives and drinking water.
- **Sampling.** Official sampling follows a rulebook that, according to the US report, applies to first-time imports, changed labels and high-risk origins.
- **Excise stamps.** Needed for alcohol, wine, non-alcoholic beverages and tobacco before import.

Goods subject to such controls may enter only through the border crossings designated for them, under an article added to the Customs Law by the 2022 amendments.

## 5. Labelling

Food on the Serbian market must carry the mandatory particulars in Serbian, and multilingual labels are allowed. The label shows the name and address of the food business operator, registered in Serbia, under whose name the food is placed on the market; responsibility lies with that operator or with the importer if the operator is not registered in Serbia. US sources describe importers applying translated stick-on labels before retail distribution. An amending rulebook reported as published in March 2026 applies in part from 1 July 2026 and adds dairy producer and meat origin particulars; check the gazette text for the transition wording.

## Confectionery and snacks

For confectionery, biscuits and snacks the same chain applies, and a few points often decide the route. First, the tariff code and the duty rate under the agreement that fits the origin. Second, the VAT rate: confectionery does not appear on the 10 percent list that was read, so 20 percent is likely, but the rate must be confirmed per tariff code. Third, the Serbian label with the importer's details. Fourth, animal-origin ingredients: a US report states that Ministry of Agriculture import approval is required for animal-origin products with more than 2 percent animal component, so dairy or egg ingredients should be checked. Exporters of confectionery and snacks to Serbia should confirm these points before the first production run for the market.

## 6. After clearance

Market inspection by the trade ministry checks quality at retail. The Law on Official Controls (Official Gazette RS 109/2025), applied from 1 June 2026, allows inspectors to visit food production and sales premises without prior notice, and labelling and declaration of food are among the control goals.

## Checklist before the first shipment

1. Tariff code, duty rate under the applicable agreement and VAT rate confirmed in writing by the importer or forwarder.
2. Importer and customs representative named, with the representation model confirmed.
3. Veterinary decision and establishment status for animal-origin goods.
4. Proof of origin if a preference is claimed (see [trade agreements for Russian exporters](../russia-serbia-trade-agreements-for-exporters/)).
5. Label in Serbian with the importer details, approved before printing.
6. Landed-cost model matching the invoice, duty and VAT actually applied.
