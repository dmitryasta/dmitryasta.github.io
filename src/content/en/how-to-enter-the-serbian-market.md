This guide follows the order in which a manufacturer or exporter usually has to take decisions. Information is current as of 30 October 2026. Rules change, and the section on each step says what to verify.

## Step 1. Decide whether the market is worth entering

Serbia has about 6.55 million inhabitants (Statistical Office estimate for 2025, based on the 2022 census). Retail is concentrated in a handful of chains, and foreign chains are reported to hold about two thirds of the market. For a food or FMCG brand the useful questions are narrower than market size: who sells comparable products today, at what shelf price, in which channel, and what margin each step takes.

A short shelf survey and calls with the trade answer these questions faster than desk research. This is the purpose of the [Serbia market research](../../services/serbia-market-research/) service and of the article on [market research before you ship](../serbia-market-research-before-you-ship/).

## Step 2. Calculate the landed cost and the shelf price

Build the price ladder from your ex-works price: freight and insurance, customs duty, import VAT, customs and handling costs, importer margin, distributor margin, retailer margin and VAT on the shelf. Two rules matter in the calculation.

- The general VAT rate is 20 percent, and a reduced rate of 10 percent applies to a list of goods. The Customs Administration list (status of 1 January 2024) includes meat, fish, milk and dairy, eggs, vegetables, fruit, cereals and flour, edible oils, sugar and bread products. Sausages, chocolate, confectionery, coffee, tea, preserved goods and juices do not appear on that list, so the rate for them must be confirmed per tariff code. Import VAT is calculated on the customs value plus duty, excise and other import charges and incidental costs up to the first destination in Serbia.
- The duty rate depends on the tariff code and on the origin of the goods. It must be read from the current customs tariff for the exact code, and not from a general table.

## Step 3. Choose the import model

A Serbian party is needed for several functions. Under the Customs Law a customs representative must generally be established in Serbia, and indirect representation is performed by a licensed freight forwarder with an authorised employee. For VAT, a non-established business that supplies goods in Serbia generally registers and appoints a fiscal representative, with exceptions for some supplies. A foreign company can also open a limited liability company (d.o.o.) or a branch, but many exporters start with a local importer or distributor.

The choice depends on volumes, margins and control over the brand. Confirm it with a lawyer and an accountant licensed in Serbia. Details of the import chain are in [importing food products into Serbia](../importing-food-products-into-serbia/).

## Step 4. Check the trade agreement for your origin

For goods of Russian origin the EAEU-Serbia agreement, signed on 25 October 2019 and in force since 10 July 2021, is the relevant instrument. Preferences require proof of origin on Form CT-2 (СТ-2 in Cyrillic) and compliance with the direct consignment rule. Goods of EU origin move under the EU-Serbia Stabilisation and Association Agreement, and goods from the CEFTA region and Turkey under their own agreements. See [Russia-Serbia trade agreements for exporters](../russia-serbia-trade-agreements-for-exporters/).

## Step 5. Prepare certification and labels

Food on the Serbian market must be declared in Serbian, and the label must carry the name and address of the food business operator registered in Serbia, or of the importer where the operator is not registered there. For animal-origin food the importer must obtain a decision of the Veterinary Directorate on veterinary-sanitary conditions before import. It is issued per product type and covers consignments for three months, extendable by three months. Each consignment also needs the original veterinary certificate of the exporting country, and consignments pass only through border crossings with veterinary control. The producing establishment must meet the prescribed conditions: registered in the EU, or approved by the Ministry as equivalent. For meat and dairy from Russia, the approval route should be confirmed with the Veterinary Directorate directly, because no published list of Russian meat or dairy establishments was found. Your Serbian importer should confirm the exact requirements for your product category with the competent authorities before production, not after.

## Step 6. Choose the channel and the partner

The main routes are retail chains, wholesalers and distributors, and specialised stores or HoReCa. Chains often buy through importers or distributors, and the choice of partner decides how fast the first listing comes. The process is described in [finding distributors in Serbia](../finding-distributors-in-serbia/) and [selling to Serbian retail chains](../selling-to-serbian-retail-chains/).

## Step 7. Know the 2026 changes

Three developments are relevant for 2026. The Law on Trade Practices for Certain Types of Products (Official Gazette RS 35/2026) regulates payment terms and unfair practices between suppliers and buyers of food and some other goods. An amending rulebook on food labelling, reported in the press as published in March 2026, applies in part from 1 July 2026: for milk and dairy products the label must also show the producer or processor if the operator is neither, and for listed meat categories not of Serbian origin the country of origin must be stated. The gazette number and the transition wording should be checked in the Official Gazette. The Law on Official Controls (Official Gazette RS 109/2025), applied from 1 June 2026, allows inspectors to visit food premises without prior notice. Read the current texts or ask your importer before you sign contracts or print labels.

## What to do next

If the numbers work, the sequence is: first shipment of a limited range, a distributor or a direct buyer, a weekly reporting rhythm, and a decision about the next three shipments.
