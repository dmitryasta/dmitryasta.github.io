A foreign manufacturer can be present in Serbia in several ways, and the right one depends on what the presence must do. This article separates the functions and describes the options. The options apply equally to a manufacturer from Russia that wants its own representation in Serbia. It is a commercial overview, not legal advice. Information is current as of 30 October 2026.

## Separate the functions

| Function | Who usually performs it |
|---|---|
| Buys, stocks and resells on its own account | Distributor or wholesaler |
| Clears the goods and carries import formalities | Importer of record with a customs representative or forwarder |
| Negotiates and supports sales on your behalf, without taking title | Commercial agent or representative |
| Reports on stock, shelf and prices, coordinates promotions | Commercial representative, local manager or consultant |
| Invoices in Serbia, employs staff, signs local contracts | Serbian legal entity or branch |

US government guidance describes selecting a suitable local partner, whether agent, distributor or representative, as critical, and strongly advises legal review with local counsel.

## Corporate forms for a foreign company

According to a law-firm guide published in January 2026 and updated in March 2026:

- **Limited liability company (d.o.o.).** A foreign founder can hold 100 percent. Capital starts from RSD 100. Registration at the Business Registers Agency takes 3 to 5 business days. Other company types exist, such as joint stock companies, with higher minimum capital.
- **Branch.** Not a separate legal entity. It may carry out commercial activity. Registration takes about 10 to 15 business days.
- **Representative office.** Not a commercial entity. Its activity is limited to research, promotion and marketing.

## What Serbian law says about agents and distributors

A law-firm guide to distribution in Serbia (CMS, updated 1 July 2025; a secondary source, with statute articles not checked) states that commercial agency agreements must be in writing, that an agency of indefinite duration can be terminated at the end of a calendar quarter with one month's notice (two months if it lasted more than three years) and that goodwill indemnity is not available, although lost commission and actual damage can be claimed if notice is unreasonable. It also states that Serbian law has no specific rules on distribution agreements, which can be oral or written, and that written form is recommended. Treat this as a reason to have the agreement drafted locally, not as a legal position.

## Customs and VAT roles

For customs, a foreign seller without a Serbian entity can be represented only by a representative established in Serbia, with exceptions. Indirect representation is performed by a licensed freight forwarder. For VAT, a non-established business that supplies goods in Serbia generally registers and appoints a fiscal representative, with exceptions. These roles are performed by licensed Serbian professionals and are not part of a commercial representative's mandate.

## The independent commercial representative

Between a distributor that buys the goods and a full local company there is a practical middle option: an independent person in Belgrade who works for the manufacturer. The usual scope is:

- regular contact with distributors, importers and buyers on your behalf;
- order planning and sales forecasts, so that the next delivery arrives before key products run out;
- a promotion calendar: proposals, agreement with partners and control of execution;
- store and warehouse visits with photos and figures;
- a weekly report on orders, stock, price changes, competitors and open issues.

The mandate should be explicit about what the representative may decide, what needs the manufacturer's approval and what is reported. Signing contracts or taking liabilities requires a separate written mandate and, if invoicing is involved, a registered entity.

## Choosing a model

1. If you need goods bought and resold: a distributor, see [finding distributors in Serbia](../finding-distributors-in-serbia/).
2. If you need control of the relationship on the ground without a company: an independent representative, see the [local commercial representation](../../services/local-commercial-representation/) service.
3. If you need to invoice in Serbia or employ staff: a d.o.o. or a branch, set up with a Serbian lawyer and accountant.
4. If you only need to promote the brand: consider a representative office, remembering that it may not trade.

The choice can change as volumes grow. A common path is a distributor first, a representative second, and a local company when volumes justify it.
