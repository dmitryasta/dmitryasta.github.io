Distributor choice is the decision with the longest consequences for a brand in Serbia. This article explains how to organise the search. Information is current as of 30 October 2026.

## Start with the channel

List the channels you want: retail chains, wholesalers, specialised stores, HoReCa, online. The Serbian retail market is concentrated: the ten largest players reported about EUR 5.0 billion of combined 2025 revenue, with the largest chains operating hundreds of stores, according to trade press. A distributor that serves these chains is a different partner from one that serves small traditional shops. The same candidate can be right for one channel and wrong for another.

## Understand the roles

The functions are often mixed in practice, and it helps to name them:

- importer of record: clears the goods and carries the import formalities;
- distributor: buys, stores, sells and delivers on its own account;
- commercial agent or representative: negotiates and supports sales on your behalf for a fee or commission, without buying the goods;
- customs representative or forwarder: lodges the customs declaration, with the licence the law requires.

US government guidance describes selecting a suitable local partner, whether agent, distributor or representative, as critical, and strongly advises legal review with local counsel. Distribution and agent commissions are listed there as a cost in the pricing structure.

## Build the long list and the short list

Sources for the long list are direct contacts in the trade, supplier lists of chains, trade fairs and public registers. The short list comes from calls and meetings. A comparison table with the same criteria for every candidate keeps the decision honest:

| Criterion | What to check |
|---|---|
| Channels and chains | Which chains and wholesalers they already deliver to, and in which regions |
| Categories | Whether they carry competing or complementary products |
| Logistics | Warehouse capacity, temperature control if needed, delivery frequency |
| Finance | Payment history, willingness to pay on agreed terms, credit exposure |
| Sales team | Size, field coverage, key account managers for chains |
| Support | Promotions, merchandising, reporting, handling of returns |
| References | Other foreign suppliers who can describe real experience |

## Negotiate the terms that matter

Agree the price list and the price ladder to the shelf, margins, payment terms, minimum purchase volumes, territory, the length of the agreement and the conditions for termination. Exclusivity should be exchanged for obligations that can be measured. A written agreement drafted or reviewed by a Serbian lawyer is the standard.

## Know what Serbian law says about agents and distributors

A law-firm guide to distribution in Serbia (CMS, last updated 1 July 2025; a secondary source, statute articles not checked) summarises the position as follows. Commercial agency agreements must be in writing. Serbian law has no specific rules on distribution agreements, which can be oral or written, and written form is recommended. Distributors are generally not entitled to goodwill indemnity, and Serbian law is not aligned with the EU Agency Directive 86/653/EEC. Exclusivity and non-compete clauses are block-exempted under competition rules when each party's market share is below 25 percent. These points are the reason to have the contract drafted locally.

## Check the Law on Trade Practices

The Law on Trade Practices for Certain Types of Products (Official Gazette RS 35/2026) applies to supplier-buyer relations in agricultural and food products and several other categories, for trade on Serbian territory. It sets maximum payment terms of 30 days for perishable agri-food products and 60 days for other agri-food products, lists practices that are always prohibited and practices presumed unfair, and presumes that charges for placing a product in the buyer's range are unfair unless conditions are met. Written contracts become mandatory under provisions that apply from 1 January 2027, as read in the legal database text. The text of the law in the legal database does not expressly address foreign suppliers, so ask your lawyer whether and how it applies to your contract.
