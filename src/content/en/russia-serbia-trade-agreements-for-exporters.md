Exporters of Russian-origin goods to Serbia often ask which agreement applies and what documents the preference needs. This article summarises what could be verified in primary and secondary sources as of 30 October 2026. It is a commercial orientation, not legal or customs advice.

## The current instrument

The Eurasian Economic Commission presents the free trade agreement between the Eurasian Economic Union and Serbia as in force. It was signed in Moscow on 25 October 2019 and entered into force on 10 July 2021. The text provides that it enters into force 60 days after the last written notification of completed internal procedures, that it is concluded for an indefinite period and that either party may terminate it by notification, with effect from the first day of the seventh month after receipt. A decision of the Board of the EEC of 6 October 2020 deals with the tariff rates applied under the agreement.

The status of the earlier bilateral agreement between Russia and the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (dated 28 August 2000) after July 2021 could not be confirmed from a primary source. For goods originating in the Union, the EAEU-Serbia agreement is the instrument to use.

## What it covers

A government source in Kazakhstan reports that the agreement eliminates import duties on 93 percent of the tariff nomenclature. According to the same secondary sources, sensitive items such as cigarettes, processed cheeses and strong alcohol are duty-free only within quotas, and duties remain on poultry meat, sugar, certain fabrics, compressors and vehicles. The Customs Administration tariff page does not list EAEU rates in a separate column, so the rate for your tariff code must be confirmed with a customs specialist or the importer.

## Proof of origin

Under the rules of origin annexed to the agreement:

- The proof is a **certificate of origin on Form CT-2**, in the form given in the annex to the rules. The English text prints "CT-2" for the form name written in Cyrillic; in Latin transliteration it is ST-2.
- It is an original in hard copy, with limited exceptions, issued by an authorised body designated by a party, and valid for **12 months**.
- A retrospective certificate must carry the note "Issued retrospectively".
- For consignments with a customs value of **up to EUR 5,000**, a declaration of origin by the producer, exporter or consignor, signed in hard copy and valid for up to 12 months, is provided instead.

A Serbian news report from the first days of the agreement states that the former requirement of a direct sale contract between residents of the two countries no longer applies, so goods can move under contracts with third-party intermediaries. The importer applying a preferential rate must present proof of origin, evidence of direct transport and, where relevant, the third-country invoice.

## The direct consignment rule

The rules define direct consignment as transportation of goods from the territory of one party into the territory of the other party without transit through the territory of any third party. The EEC reported on 29 May 2026 that the Supreme Eurasian Economic Council approved negotiations with Serbia to amend the agreement in order to resolve difficulties in proving compliance with the direct shipment rule, under which goods must remain unchanged during transit and supporting documents are presented to customs. The scope of the change was not published in the material read. Until an amendment is in force, plan the route and the documents with your forwarder and the Serbian importer, and do not assume that an exception applies.

## Other arrangements

Goods that do not originate in the Union fall under other arrangements. Serbia applies the EU-Serbia Stabilisation and Association Agreement (in force since 1 September 2013, after an interim trade agreement from 1 February 2010), the CEFTA agreement and a free trade agreement with Turkey (in force since 1 September 2010). Under the EU agreement, origin is proved with EUR.1 certificates or origin declarations under transitional rules. These instruments are not interchangeable with the EAEU certificate.

An EU spokesperson stated in October 2019 that Serbia had committed to withdraw from bilateral free trade agreements on the day of accession, a general rule for candidate countries. The statement as reported did not say whether the EAEU agreement falls under it. Nothing was found indicating a scheduled change, but exporters planning long-term contracts should note that the framework depends on the political process.

## Tariffs are not the only barrier

- **Veterinary approval.** The importer must obtain a decision of the Veterinary Directorate for animal-origin goods, and the producing establishment must meet the prescribed conditions. The list of approved establishments for the Russian Federation published on the Directorate site covers fish and fishery products only; no published list of Russian meat or dairy establishments was found. The route for meat and dairy producers should be confirmed with the Directorate. See [importing food products into Serbia](../importing-food-products-into-serbia/).
- **Quantitative measures can appear and disappear.** Quantitative restrictions on vegetable oils and margarines were introduced in March 2024 and lifted on 4 March 2025; reports said EAEU exports of sunflower oil and margarine to Serbia fell by about 40 percent over ten months after they began.
- **Labelling and VAT.** Labels in Serbian with the importer's details, and import VAT at 20 or 10 percent on the customs value plus duty.
- **Logistics.** EU sanctions prohibit road transport undertakings established in Russia or Belarus from carrying goods by road within the EU, and extend to Russian-registered trailers, with individual derogations. The version read dates from June 2023, does not mention Serbia by name, and no current source on routes between Russia and Serbia was found. Confirm the route and the carrier with your forwarder before you quote delivery terms.

## Practical steps

1. Establish the origin of the product under the rules of origin, and keep production records.
2. Agree with the Serbian importer who is responsible for proof of origin and in what form.
3. Choose the route and the carrier with direct consignment and sanctions rules in mind.
4. Confirm the duty rate, VAT rate and veterinary status for the tariff code in writing.
5. Build the landed-cost model with the preference only if the documents are certain ([market research before you ship](../serbia-market-research-before-you-ship/)).
