This article explains how Serbian grocery retail is organised and which legal rules govern the relationship between suppliers and chains in 2026. It answers two practical questions: how a supplier gets into Serbian retail chains, and how to offer a product to a Serbian retailer. Information is current as of 30 October 2026. Commercial terms such as listing fees and promotional contributions are not published, and the article says so where that is the case.

## The structure of the market

Trade press reports the ten largest retail players by 2025 financial data as follows. The article mixes net revenue, turnover and total revenue, and gives no market shares, so the figures show scale rather than a ranking by like-for-like measures.

|---|---|---|
| Delhaize Serbia (Maxi) | EUR 1.47 billion | more than 500 |
| Lidl Srbija | EUR 1.08 billion | more than 80 |
| Idea Marketi | EUR 793 million | over 300 |
| Univerexport | EUR 379 million | over 200 |
| Metro Cash and Carry | EUR 362 million | not stated |
| Aman | EUR 303 million | more than 280 |

Companies are named here only to describe the market. This does not mean that Dmitry Astakhov has an agreement with, or represents, any of them.

The ten together reported about EUR 5.0 billion. US government guidance states that foreign chains hold about two thirds of the retail market, and the same report says retail sales turnover grew 5.6 percent year on year in the first seven months of 2025. The Statistical Office reports total retail turnover for January to September 2026 up 9.4 percent at current prices and 7.4 percent at constant prices compared with the same period of 2025; this is total retail, not a food series.

Consolidation is under way: Aman agreed in April 2026 to take over DIS. An older competition inquiry (covering 2017 and 2018) found that traditional outlets up to 200 square metres were most of the shops but a minority of revenue, a reminder that small stores and wholesalers remain a separate channel.

## The legal frame in 2026

### Law on Trade Practices for Certain Types of Products

The law, published in Official Gazette RS 35/2026, regulates supplier-buyer relations in agricultural and food products and in several other categories, including household chemicals, paper goods, personal hygiene and cosmetics, diapers and plant protection products. It applies to relations on Serbian territory regardless of the law governing the contract. Its main elements:

- **Payment terms.** 30 days for perishable agri-food products and 60 days for other agri-food products.
- **Prohibited practices (black list).** Unilateral change of key terms, short-notice cancellation of ordered perishables, payments unrelated to sales and charging for goods lost without the supplier's fault.
- **Presumed unfair practices (grey list).** Returning unsold goods without payment, storage fees and unilateral cuts of orders or termination without at least 30 days' notice, unless the buyer proves otherwise.
- **Listing fees.** Charges for placing a product in the buyer's range or for administrative costs of starting cooperation are presumed unfair, except where the supplier requested the service and the fee reflects actual costs or objective pre-agreed criteria.
- **Retaliation.** Commercial retaliation against a supplier is prohibited.
- **Who is protected.** A buyer is presumed to have significant bargaining power where the revenues of supplier and buyer in Serbia fall within thresholds set in the law; an example in the text is a supplier under EUR 2,000,000 and a buyer over EUR 2,000,000.
- **Written contracts.** The provisions requiring written contracts apply from 1 January 2027.
- **Enforcement.** Fines for legal entities range from RSD 50,000 to 2,000,000 for some violations and RSD 300,000 for failing to conclude a written contract by the deadline. The Commission for Protection of Competition may impose measures based on annual revenue. A Commission instruction on assessing unfair practices applies from 4 July 2026.

The text read in the legal database does not expressly address foreign suppliers. Whether and how it applies to a foreign manufacturer selling through an importer is a question for a Serbian lawyer.

### Amendments to the Law on Trade

The April 2026 amendments add registration of purchasing points for agricultural products, unit-price display, rules for promotional sales (limited to new products, up to 60 days) and a reference period for the previous price in discounts. They contain no new supplier-payment obligations.

### The margin decree

A Government decree capped retail margins at 20 percent for 23 categories from 1 September 2025 for six months. A revision from 29 November 2025 capped retailer fees to suppliers, such as marketing or listing fees, at 10 percent of the supplier's net invoiced amount and barred delisting without written consent. The decree ceased to apply on 1 March 2026 and no replacement was found. Pricing models from the winter of 2025 and 2026 should not be reused without checking.

## How to prepare a chain meeting

1. Know your landed cost and the margin ladder to the shelf, and decide your floor price (see [market research before you ship](../serbia-market-research-before-you-ship/)).
2. Decide who invoices the chain: you, an importer or a distributor.
3. Prepare the dossier: product specification, certificates, Serbian label, price list, logistics terms, shelf life and minimum order.
4. Prepare a promotion proposal with its cost and who carries it.
5. Ask for payment terms in writing and compare them with the legal limits above.
6. Plan reporting after the listing: sales, stock and shelf checks, which is the role of [local commercial representation](../local-commercial-representation-in-serbia/).
